AI, explained softly

AI is in our phones, classrooms and feeds, yet it is usually explained as magic or doom. This page tries something softer: plain words, real examples, honest uncertainty.

01 · the basics

What is AI, really?

AI is software that learns from examples instead of following only hand-written rules.

Fact The OECD describes an AI system as a machine that works out, from its input, how to produce outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions.

In practice, it spots patterns in millions of examples, then guesses: a chatbot predicts a likely next word; an image generator refines random noise into a picture.

Fact Those examples come from us: Google's C4 training dataset was built from about 15 million websites.

My view AI does not “understand” like people do. It is brilliant at patterns, which is why it can sound confident and still be wrong.

02 · the good

Where AI helps

  • 🧬

    Science

    Fact DeepMind's AlphaFold has predicted the 3D shapes of over 200 million proteins, helping research into diseases and medicines. It shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

  • 📚

    Studying

    My view I use AI to quiz myself, re-explain hard concepts and find gaps in my notes: a great study buddy, a bad ghostwriter.

  • 💼

    Work

    My view Drafting, summarising, translating and coding get faster, if a person checks the result.

  • 🦻

    Accessibility

    My view Live captions, voice control and image descriptions help people who are deaf, blind or have limited mobility.

03 · the tricky

Where it goes wrong

  • 🙈

    Errors

    Fact Chatbots can invent facts and sources in a confident tone. NIST calls this “confabulation”.

  • ⚖️

    Bias

    Fact In the Gender Shades study, face-analysis tools misclassified darker-skinned women up to 34.7% of the time, versus 0.8% for lighter-skinned men.

  • 🔒

    Privacy

    My view Training data is scraped at huge scale, so personal posts and photos can end up inside AI without consent.

  • 🎭

    Deepfakes

    Fact Google told Australia's regulator it received 258 reports of suspected terrorist deepfakes made with its Gemini AI in under a year.

Hong Kong · 2024

The video call where everyone was fake

Fact An employee joined a video call with the company's “finance chief” and colleagues. All were deepfakes. The employee sent scammers HK$200 million (US$25.6 million).

My view Lesson: for money or passwords, double-check through another channel.

AI agents · July 2026

The agent that left its sandbox

Fact In OpenAI's internal security tests, AI agents with weakened safeguards escaped their sandbox and broke into Hugging Face's systems, taking credentials. Reuters reported OpenAI took at least a week to link the attack to its agents. OpenAI disputes parts of that.

My view Lesson: agents need tight limits, monitoring and an incident plan.

Outages · 2025

When the cloud blinks

Fact In November 2025, a settings change at Cloudflare took big parts of the web offline for hours. In December, Amazon's AI coding agent Kiro deleted and recreated an environment, interrupting an AWS service for 13 hours; Amazon blamed human access settings.

My view Lesson: a few companies hold up much of the internet, so automated changes need human review.
