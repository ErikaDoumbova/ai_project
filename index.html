01 · the basics

What is AI, really?

AI is software that learns from examples instead of only following rules a programmer wrote by hand.

Fact The OECD describes an AI system as a machine that works out, from the input it receives, how to produce outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions.

In practice: show a computer millions of examples, let it adjust itself until it spots the patterns, then ask it to guess. A chatbot guesses a likely next word. An image generator starts from random noise and cleans it up, step by step, until it matches your prompt.

Fact Those examples come from us. Google's C4 dataset, used to train language models, was built from about 15 million websites, from Wikipedia to personal blogs.

My view AI does not “understand” the world the way people do. It is brilliant at patterns, which is exactly why it can sound confident and still be wrong.

02 · the good

Where AI helps

  • 🧬

    Science

    Fact DeepMind's AlphaFold has predicted the 3D shapes of over 200 million proteins, helping researchers study disease and design medicines. The work shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

  • 📚

    Studying

    My view I use AI to quiz myself before exams, get a second explanation of a hard concept and spot gaps in my notes. It is a great study buddy and a bad ghostwriter.

  • 💼

    Work

    My view Drafting emails, summarising long reports, translating and writing code all get faster, as long as a person checks the result before it goes out.

  • 🦻

    Accessibility

    My view Live captions, voice control and automatic image descriptions can make technology easier to use for people who are deaf, blind or have limited mobility.
